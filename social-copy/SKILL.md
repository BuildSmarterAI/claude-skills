# Social Copy

Social copy competes with everything else on the feed, and loses by default. The hook is most of the work; the rest either pays it off or wastes it.

## When to Activate

- Writing an organic post for any social platform.
- Hooks, captions, threads, short-video scripts.
- Adapting one idea across platforms without flattening it into the same post four times.
- Rewriting a post that reads as corporate.

## When NOT to Activate

- Paid ad creative -> `ad-copy`.
- Multi-platform content *systems*, calendars, and repurposing pipelines -> `content-engine`.
- Long-form articles and newsletters -> `article-writing`.
- Establishing the voice profile itself -> `brand-voice`.

## Inputs required

The platform, the account's voice and who posts as it (person or brand), the single purpose of this post, what the audience already knows, and the proof inventory. Social posts frequently need no offer - but they always need a purpose.

## One purpose per post

Name it before writing. A post trying to do two of these does neither.

- **Demonstrate** - show competence by being useful in public
- **Position** - stake a claim about how the work should be done
- **Prove** - one specific result or story, concretely
- **Provoke** - contest a widely held belief in the field
- **Invite** - ask for a specific response
- **Report** - something happened, and it is worth knowing

## The hook

The first line is the whole decision. Everything after it is read only by people the hook kept.

**Hooks that work:**
- A specific situation the reader recognises immediately
- A number that does not sound rounded
- A stated position someone could disagree with
- A short admission of something that went wrong
- A concrete moment, mid-scene

**Hooks that do not:**
- "In today's fast-paced world"
- A rhetorical question the reader can answer "no" to and scroll
- Announcing what the post will be about
- "I'm excited to share"
- Anything that could open a post from any account in the category

Write the hook last, from the strongest line already in the draft. The best hook is usually a sentence that was buried in paragraph three.

## Platform register

| Platform | Register | Shape | Do not |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn** | Professional but personal; first person | Hook, line break, short paragraphs, one takeaway | Engagement bait, false vulnerability, "agree?" |
| **X** | Compressed, direct, opinionated | One idea per post; threads only when the argument needs steps | Padding a thread to hit a number |
| **Instagram** | Visual-first; caption supports the image | Hook in the first two lines before truncation | Caption that ignores the image |
| **TikTok / Shorts / Reels** | Spoken, native, fast | Hook in the first two seconds; script for the ear | Reading written copy aloud |
| **Facebook** | Conversational, community | Plain, direct, low polish | Corporate announcements |
| **YouTube** | Title and description do search work; script does retention | Title states the payoff; script front-loads it | Withholding the payoff to pad watch time |
| **Threads** | Casual, conversational, reply-oriented | Short, opinionated, invites response | Cross-posted LinkedIn formality |

Adapting across platforms means rewriting for the register, not reposting with different hashtags.

## Post structure

```
HOOK        first line - the only line guaranteed to be read
CONTEXT     one or two lines of situation
SUBSTANCE   the actual thing: the method, the result, the argument
TURN        what it means, or what changed
CLOSE       a question worth answering, or nothing at all
```

Not every post needs a CTA. A post that is useful and asks for nothing builds more than a post that asks every time. Where there is an ask, make it one thing.

## Craft rules

- **Line breaks are punctuation on social.** Dense blocks do not get read.
- **Specifics over categories.** "The estimator on the Tuesday walk-through" beats "stakeholders".
- **Do not bury the point** to manufacture a scroll. Readers punish this.
- **No engagement bait.** "Comment YES", "agree?", "unpopular opinion:" on an entirely popular opinion.
- **Emoji per the account's actual voice**, not as decoration.
- **Hashtags** only where the platform still uses them for discovery, and only ones that are real.
- **If the account posts as a person, write as that person.** Do not write a corporate post and attach a face.

## Short-video scripts

- Hook in the first two seconds, spoken.
- Write for the ear: contractions, shorter clauses, one clause per breath.
- State the payoff early; retention comes from delivering, not from withholding.
- Mark on-screen text separately from spoken lines.
- Say what the viewer should do only if there is something worth doing.

## Output contract

```
PLATFORM: <...>   PURPOSE: <demonstrate | position | prove | provoke | invite | report>
POSTING AS: <person | brand>

HOOK OPTIONS
  1. <text>   2. <text>   3. <text>

POST
  <full text, with line breaks as they should appear>

  Claims:  <[FACT] source | [PROPOSED] | [NEEDS-INPUT]>
  CTA:     <one action, or NONE - deliberately>

ADAPTATIONS  (if multi-platform)
  <platform>: <rewritten for register - not the same text>

NOTES
  Character count vs platform limit: <n>/<limit>
  Voice source: <brand-voice profile | supplied samples | NEEDS-INPUT>
```

## Quality gates

- One purpose, stated.
- The hook is a line someone would stop for, and could not open a competitor's post.
- Every claim traces to the proof inventory or is a `[NEEDS-INPUT]` slot.
- Platform register matches; adaptations are rewrites, not reposts.
- No engagement bait, no manufactured vulnerability, no invented anecdote.
- Line breaks and length suit the platform.
- If the post asks for something, it asks for exactly one thing.

## Failure conditions

- The post needs a personal story and none is available: propose the structure and mark the story a `[NEEDS-INPUT]` slot. Never write a plausible anecdote.
- The account has no voice profile and posts as a named person: ask for samples, or write plainly and flag that voice fidelity is unverified.
- The purpose is "engagement" rather than one of the six: push back - that is not a purpose, and it is what produces bait.

## Handoffs

`copy-strategist` when the post supports a campaign · `brand-voice` for the profile · `content-engine` for calendars and repurposing systems · `copychief` for campaign-tied posts · `humanizer` before publishing · `compliance-review` where a post makes a claim, comparison, or endorsement.

## Provenance rule

Never invent an anecdote, a client story, a result, a conversation, or a statistic to make a post land. An unverifiable story is a `[NEEDS-INPUT]` slot. Full contract: `copy-os/references/fact-provenance.md`.
